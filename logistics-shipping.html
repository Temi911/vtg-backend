Choose the right movement

Shipping support shaped around your cargo.

Every shipment has different deadlines, handling needs, paperwork and costs. These service areas help trade participants understand the decisions to coordinate before goods move.

01 · Ocean transport

Sea freight and container shipping

Plan the movement of commercial cargo by sea, including shipment origin, destination port, cargo readiness, container or consolidation needs, and the milestones that matter along the way. Sea freight can suit larger consignments and goods that are less time-sensitive, subject to the carrier, route and cargo requirements.

  • Origin and destination planning
  • Container and consolidated-cargo coordination
  • Departure, transit and arrival milestones
02 · Time-sensitive movement

Air freight and urgent consignments

For cargo where speed is a priority, compare air-shipping requirements with the shipment's value, dimensions, weight, handling conditions and delivery deadline. Actual availability, transit times, rates and restrictions must be confirmed with the appointed carrier or logistics provider.

  • Shipment dimensions and weight readiness
  • Handling and carrier requirements
  • Arrival planning and receiving arrangements
03 · Before dispatch

Pre-shipment inspection and cargo readiness

Help buyers define what should be checked before goods leave the supplier: product identity, quantity, visible condition, packaging, labels and agreed specifications. Where a VTG Agent inspection is arranged, photos and supporting evidence can be associated with the relevant order or shipment record.

  • Inspection scope agreed in advance
  • Photo evidence and document collection
  • Clear records of observations and exceptions
04 · Arrival and delivery

Port handling, customs and inland movement

Coordinate the information required for arrival, customs processing, cargo release and onward transport. Import requirements differ by country, product and transaction; buyers should confirm applicable permits, duties, taxes, declarations and deadlines with their licensed clearing agent and relevant authorities.

  • Arrival and release milestone tracking
  • Document and clearance coordination
  • Destination, receiving and handover planning

Who it helps

A shared view for every trade participant.

Logistics works best when the buyer, supplier and appointed service partners can see the information relevant to their role.

For buyers and importers

Understand the planned shipping journey, prepare receiving arrangements, keep track of required documents and follow recorded shipment milestones without having to piece together every update from separate conversations.

For suppliers and exporters

Coordinate cargo readiness, packaging details, inspection arrangements, export paperwork and dispatch information so the buyer and logistics provider can work from consistent shipment details.

For logistics and clearing partners

Share relevant route, cargo, document and milestone information with authorized participants. Service scope, responsibilities and timing should be confirmed directly with the appointed provider.

Prepare for a smoother shipment

What to have ready before you request support.

Good shipment information helps providers assess the request and identify what still needs confirmation.

Product and cargo details

Prepare a clear goods description, quantity, approximate weight and dimensions, packaging details, photographs if available, and any special handling requirements.

Origin and destination

Identify the supplier's city or collection point, preferred origin port or airport if known, destination country and port, and final delivery location.

Trade and shipping documents

Gather available commercial invoices, packing lists, purchase or order references, transport documents, certificates and permits relevant to the cargo and route.

Timing and service expectations

State when the goods will be ready, your target delivery window, the preferred transport mode and any important receiving or clearance deadlines.

The continental picture

Two systems changing how Africa trades

These are the structures buyers and suppliers keep hearing about. Here is what they are and what has, and has not, been delivered.

AfCFTA, the African Continental Free Trade Area

Signed in 2018 and in force since 1 January 2021, it covers 54 signatory nations and about 1.4 billion people, with its secretariat in Accra.

  • The goods protocol aims to remove tariffs on 90% of goods over a phased period
  • Rules of origin cover most product categories; gaps remain in textiles, processed foods and some industrial products
  • The Guided Trade Initiative began in October 2022 with 8 countries; volumes are described as modest
  • The World Bank estimates intra-African trade could rise about 52% by 2035 if fully implemented

PAPSS, the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System

Built by Afreximbank with the African Union and the AfCFTA secretariat, so African buyers and sellers can settle in local currency instead of routing through the US dollar.

  • Reports 28 countries and 190+ banks and fintechs
  • The BEAC (CEMAC) region is reported as not yet fully operational
  • A BCEAO pilot is scheduled, not started
  • The $5bn yearly fee saving is the source’s estimate

The import path into Nigeria

The official sequence importers follow, as published by Nigeria’s National Single Window. Some steps overlap and can run in parallel, and a licensed customs agent is required for clearance.

  1. Register as an importerIncorporate with the Corporate Affairs Commission, get a TIN from the Nigeria Revenue Service, and register on the National Single Window, B’odogwu and Tax Promax.
  2. Check the HS codeConfirm duty rates, levies such as CET and CISS, and which agencies apply to your product.
  3. Get licences and certificatesNAFDAC for food, drugs and chemicals, SON (SONCAP), NESREA for environmental permits, and others by product.
  4. Open Form MApply through an authorised dealer bank on the B’odogwu platform.
  5. Get the PAARThe Pre-Arrival Assessment Report comes from the Nigeria Customs Service through B’odogwu.
  6. Clear the goodsSubmit the declaration, pay duties, VAT and levies, then wait for inspection and a release order.

Know who you are buying from

Different supplier types serve different purposes.

A supplier's category can affect pricing, customization, minimum order quantities, production control and communication.

Manufacturer

Produces goods directly or controls production. May suit larger orders, custom manufacturing and repeat supply.

Wholesaler / Distributor

May hold or source finished goods from multiple producers. Useful for variety, ready stock or smaller quantities.

Trading Company

May coordinate products from several factories. Ask who actually manufactures the goods and who invoices you.

Brand Owner

Controls branded products and may impose distribution, branding, warranty or territory requirements.

Export Supplier

May specialize in international transactions. Confirm whether it is the manufacturer, intermediary or both.

Ask the role

Do not assume a profile tells the whole story. Understand who makes, invoices and stands behind the agreed specifications.

Supplier capability

Look beyond the quotation.

A supplier can have an attractive price and still be unsuitable. Evaluate capabilities that matter to your product and market.

Production capacity

Ask about current capacity, peak periods and whether the supplier can consistently produce the required volume.

MOQ & flexibility

Confirm minimum order quantity, price breaks, sample availability and trial-order options.

Lead time

Separate sample, production, dispatch and transport time. Production lead time is not delivery time.

Communication

Clear written communication matters for specifications, packaging, quality standards and changes.

Compliance

Where relevant, ask which certifications, test reports, licences or product standards apply.

Export readiness

Confirm experience preparing goods and documents for the destination market and chosen shipping method.

Sourcing requests

You do not always need to know the supplier first.

A structured sourcing request gives VTG and potential suppliers enough information to identify relevant options.

Give the sourcing team

Product and intended use.
Exact specifications, material, model or grade.
Quantity and whether it is trial or repeat supply.
Branding, packaging or customization requirements.
Destination and relevant compliance needs.
Timing and delivery constraints.

A useful sourcing response should clarify

Supplier identity and role.
Product and specification match.
MOQ, quotation basis and lead time.
Customization and sample options.
Relevant supporting documents.
Known limitations or conditions.
Compare suppliers properly

The cheapest quote is not automatically the best deal.

Compare the complete commercial and operational requirement. A lower unit price can be outweighed by MOQ, quality, delays, packaging, logistics or poor communication.

Samples & production approval

Approve what you expect to receive.

For products where quality or specifications matter materially, a sample or pre-production approval can create a clearer reference point before volume production.

Sample review

Check dimensions, material, finish, function, packaging and other characteristics that matter to the final order.

Written approval

Record approved specifications and agreed changes instead of relying only on informal conversations.

Mass production

For custom products, confirm production follows the approved specification, sample and packaging requirements.

OEM, ODM & private label

Custom sourcing needs a different conversation.

Address design, specifications, samples, packaging, branding and production approval before committing to volume.

OEM

Products manufactured to the buyer's specified design, requirements or branding arrangements, subject to supplier capability and agreement.

ODM

A supplier may provide an existing or developed product adapted to agreed requirements and branding.

Private label

Existing products may be supplied under the buyer's brand with agreed packaging, labels and compliance requirements.

Questions before commitment

Make the important details explicit.

Ask the supplier

Who actually manufactures the goods?
What exactly is included in the quotation?
What is the MOQ and production capacity?
Can a sample be provided?
What happens if goods do not match the agreed specification?
What warranty, replacement or after-sales terms apply, if any?

Keep your own record

Supplier identity and contacts.
Quotation and agreed price.
Final specifications and sample reference.
Quantity, packaging and delivery requirements.
Payment and shipping terms.
Inspection or quality evidence where used.
Sourcing continuity

One supplier should not always be your only plan.

For important or repeat supply, understanding alternatives can reduce disruption when capacity, quality, production or regulatory conditions change.

Alternative suppliers

Identify comparable suppliers where practical for critical or recurring products.

Continuity risks

Shutdowns, shortages, quality deterioration, regulatory changes or extended lead times can affect supply.

Re-source deliberately

Re-check specifications, samples, pricing, documents and terms rather than assuming a replacement is equivalent.

Frequently asked

Questions about sourcing through VTG.

Can VTG find a supplier if I cannot find one?

VTG can support sourcing workflows where the relevant service is available. Provide a clear product requirement so suitable options can be assessed.

Does a supplier profile mean the supplier is risk-free?

No. Profile information or verification can provide useful evidence, but it is not a guarantee of future performance, product quality or commercial outcome.

Can I request OEM, ODM or private-label products?

Where the supplier supports it, yes. Custom sourcing should include clear specifications, sample or prototype approval, branding and packaging requirements before production.

Should I compare several suppliers?

For material purchases, comparing suitable alternatives can reveal differences in price, MOQ, quality, capacity, lead time and terms.

What if my supplier stops producing?

VTG can support a re-sourcing workflow where available. Any replacement supplier should be assessed against the requirements again.

Does VTG guarantee the supplier?

No. VTG may facilitate sourcing, information, verification and coordination, but supplier obligations and product outcomes remain subject to the applicable agreement and responsible parties.

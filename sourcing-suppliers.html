Know who you are buying from

Different supplier types serve different purposes.

A supplier's category can affect pricing, customization, minimum order quantities, production control and communication.

Manufacturer

Produces goods directly or controls production. May suit larger orders, custom manufacturing and repeat supply.

Wholesaler / Distributor

May hold or source finished goods from multiple producers. Useful for variety, ready stock or smaller quantities.

Trading Company

May coordinate products from several factories. Ask who actually manufactures the goods and who invoices you.

Brand Owner

Controls branded products and may impose distribution, branding, warranty or territory requirements.

Export Supplier

May specialize in international transactions. Confirm whether it is the manufacturer, intermediary or both.

Ask the role

Do not assume a profile tells the whole story. Understand who makes, invoices and stands behind the agreed specifications.

Supplier capability

Look beyond the quotation.

A supplier can have an attractive price and still be unsuitable. Evaluate capabilities that matter to your product and market.

Production capacity

Ask about current capacity, peak periods and whether the supplier can consistently produce the required volume.

MOQ & flexibility

Confirm minimum order quantity, price breaks, sample availability and trial-order options.

Lead time

Separate sample, production, dispatch and transport time. Production lead time is not delivery time.

Communication

Clear written communication matters for specifications, packaging, quality standards and changes.

Compliance

Where relevant, ask which certifications, test reports, licences or product standards apply.

Export readiness

Confirm experience preparing goods and documents for the destination market and chosen shipping method.

Sourcing requests

You do not always need to know the supplier first.

A structured sourcing request gives VTG and potential suppliers enough information to identify relevant options.

01 · Your request

Give the sourcing team

Share enough detail for the team to identify and assess suitable supply options.

  1. 01Product and intended useWhat you need and what the product will be used for.
  2. 02Exact specificationsMaterial, model, grade, dimensions or performance requirements.
  3. 03Quantity and order typeEstimated volume and whether this is a trial or repeat supply.
  4. 04Branding and customizationPackaging, labels, private branding or other changes required.
  5. 05Destination and complianceDelivery country and any known product or import requirements.
  6. 06Timing and deliveryTarget dates, urgency and any delivery constraints.
02 · Supplier assessment

A useful sourcing response should clarify

Each option should be presented with clear commercial details—not just a product photo and price.

  1. 01Supplier identity and roleWho the supplier is and whether it manufactures, distributes or trades.
  2. 02Product and specification matchHow closely the offer meets the requested product requirements.
  3. 03MOQ, quotation and lead timeMinimum order, pricing basis, currency, inclusions and timing.
  4. 04Customization and samplesAvailable options, sample terms and approval process.
  5. 05Supporting documentsRelevant product records, certificates or test documents, where applicable.
  6. 06Limitations and conditionsCapacity constraints, exclusions, assumptions or other conditions to note.
Compare suppliers properly

The cheapest quote is not automatically the best deal.

Compare the complete commercial and operational requirement. A lower unit price can be outweighed by MOQ, quality, delays, packaging, logistics or poor communication.

01 · Commercial fit

Compare the offer

Understand the full price and supply commitment before choosing a supplier.

  1. 01PriceUnit price, quotation basis, currency and inclusions.
  2. 02MOQMinimum quantity and trial-order flexibility.
  3. 03Lead timeSample, production, dispatch and movement timing.
  4. 04DocumentationInvoices, certificates and applicable trade records.
02 · Delivery capability

Assess what the supplier can deliver

Look at product standards, production capacity and the ability to support the relationship over time.

  1. 05QualitySpecification, materials, grade, sample and controls.
  2. 06CapacityAbility to produce the required quantity consistently.
  3. 07CustomizationOEM, ODM, private label and packaging capability.
  4. 08ReliabilityCommunication, consistency and repeat-order support.
Samples & production approval

Approve what you expect to receive.

For products where quality or specifications matter materially, a sample or pre-production approval can create a clearer reference point before volume production.

Sample review

Check dimensions, material, finish, function, packaging and other characteristics that matter to the final order.

Written approval

Record approved specifications and agreed changes instead of relying only on informal conversations.

Mass production

For custom products, confirm production follows the approved specification, sample and packaging requirements.

OEM, ODM & private label

Custom sourcing needs a different conversation.

Address design, specifications, samples, packaging, branding and production approval before committing to volume.

OEM

Products manufactured to the buyer's specified design, requirements or branding arrangements, subject to supplier capability and agreement.

ODM

A supplier may provide an existing or developed product adapted to agreed requirements and branding.

Private label

Existing products may be supplied under the buyer's brand with agreed packaging, labels and compliance requirements.

Questions before commitment

Make the important details explicit.

01 · Due diligence

Ask the supplier

Use these questions to clarify who you are dealing with and what the supplier is agreeing to provide.

  1. 01Who actually manufactures the goods?Confirm whether the contact is the factory, a distributor or a trading company.
  2. 02What exactly is included in the quotation?Clarify specifications, packaging, tooling, delivery and any exclusions.
  3. 03What is the MOQ and production capacity?Check minimum order quantities and the ability to meet your volume.
  4. 04Can a sample be provided?Agree sample cost, timing and how approval will be recorded.
  5. 05What happens if goods do not match the agreed specification?Ask about inspection, notice periods and agreed corrective steps.
  6. 06What warranty, replacement or after-sales terms apply?Record any applicable coverage, conditions and limitations.
02 · Buyer records

Keep your own record

Maintain a clear written reference for the order, approvals and any later questions or disputes.

  1. 01Supplier identity and contactsKeep the legal or trading name and the relevant contact details.
  2. 02Quotation and agreed priceSave the dated quotation, currency, pricing basis and inclusions.
  3. 03Final specifications and sample referenceRetain the approved details, sample images or reference numbers.
  4. 04Quantity, packaging and delivery requirementsRecord the agreed order quantity, packing details and destination.
  5. 05Payment and shipping termsKeep the agreed payment schedule, shipping basis and responsibilities.
  6. 06Inspection or quality evidenceFile reports, photos or other quality records where used.
Sourcing continuity

One supplier should not always be your only plan.

For important or repeat supply, understanding alternatives can reduce disruption when capacity, quality, production or regulatory conditions change.

Alternative suppliers

Identify comparable suppliers where practical for critical or recurring products.

Continuity risks

Shutdowns, shortages, quality deterioration, regulatory changes or extended lead times can affect supply.

Re-source deliberately

Re-check specifications, samples, pricing, documents and terms rather than assuming a replacement is equivalent.

Sourcing risk

Know what VTG can coordinate—and what it cannot guarantee.

VTG can support discovery, communication, workflow, verification and connections to trade services. Underlying commercial responsibilities depend on the transaction and responsible party.

01 · Product & production

Who is responsible for the goods?

Agree the product requirements and understand the supplier's production obligations before an order proceeds.

  1. 01Product specificationResponsible: Supplier and buyer, by agreement. Action: Confirm specifications in writing before production.
  2. 02Manufacturing qualityResponsible: Supplier, subject to contract. Action: Use samples and suitable inspection where appropriate.
  3. 03Supplier capacityResponsible: Supplier. Action: Confirm production capacity and lead time before commitment.
  4. 04Inspection findingsResponsible: Inspection provider, within the agreed scope. Action: Review evidence against the agreed order.
02 · Trade & accountability

Understand the wider transaction

Shipping, compliance and contractual outcomes involve different parties. Clarify responsibilities and limits in advance.

  1. 05Shipment loss or damageResponsible: Carrier, insurer or other responsible party, depending on the terms. Action: Understand shipping terms and insurance.
  2. 06Import complianceResponsible: Importer/exporter and appointed professionals. Action: Check destination requirements before ordering.
  3. 07Supplier non-performanceResponsible: Contracting parties. Action: Use the remedies and dispute mechanisms agreed in the contract.
  4. 08VTG sourcing assistanceResponsible: VTG within its agreed role. Important: VTG does not automatically guarantee supplier performance.

Frequently asked

Questions about sourcing through VTG.

Can VTG find a supplier if I cannot find one?

VTG can support sourcing workflows where the relevant service is available. Provide a clear product requirement so suitable options can be assessed.

Does a supplier profile mean the supplier is risk-free?

No. Profile information or verification can provide useful evidence, but it is not a guarantee of future performance, product quality or commercial outcome.

Can I request OEM, ODM or private-label products?

Where the supplier supports it, yes. Custom sourcing should include clear specifications, sample or prototype approval, branding and packaging requirements before production.

Should I compare several suppliers?

For material purchases, comparing suitable alternatives can reveal differences in price, MOQ, quality, capacity, lead time and terms.

What if my supplier stops producing?

VTG can support a re-sourcing workflow where available. Any replacement supplier should be assessed against the requirements again.

Does VTG guarantee the supplier?

No. VTG may facilitate sourcing, information, verification and coordination, but supplier obligations and product outcomes remain subject to the applicable agreement and responsible parties.

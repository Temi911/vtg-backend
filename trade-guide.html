Before ordering

Know what you are agreeing to.

A clear order reduces avoidable misunderstandings. Confirm the commercial details before committing funds or production.

Product details

  • Exact product and specifications
  • Quality, grade, material or model
  • Packaging and branding requirements
  • Photos, drawings or reference samples where needed

Commercial terms

  • Unit price and total value
  • Currency
  • MOQ and quantity
  • Production lead time
  • Payment terms

Delivery terms

  • Origin and destination
  • Shipping method
  • Applicable delivery terms / Incoterms
  • Insurance responsibilities
  • Expected logistics costs
Ordering options

Small quantities, samples or bulk.

VTG should not assume every supplier uses the same minimum order quantity. Availability, MOQ, pricing and customization depend on the product and supplier.

Trial & smaller orders

Start smaller where the supplier allows it.

Ask whether samples, trial quantities or smaller batches are available. A smaller order can help a buyer evaluate product quality, packaging and supplier performance before committing to larger volume.

Supplier & product checks

Questions worth asking before payment.

The right questions depend on the product, but these are useful starting points for an international purchase.

Ask the supplier

What is the exact MOQ and can it be negotiated?
What is included in the quoted price?
Where will the goods ship from?
What are the production and dispatch times?
Which certifications or compliance documents apply?
Can a sample or pre-production sample be provided?
What packaging, dimensions and weights should be expected?

For higher-value or higher-risk orders

Consider supplier verification where appropriate.
Consider an inspection before shipment.
Confirm specifications in writing.
Keep quotations, invoices and agreed terms together.
Confirm who is responsible for shipping and insurance.
Check destination-country import requirements before committing.
Important: Supplier verification or inspection can reduce uncertainty, but neither is a guarantee that every defect, delay, loss or commercial dispute will be prevented.
Documents & requirements

What documents may be involved?

There is no single document list for every trade. Requirements can change based on the product, origin, destination, transport method, value and applicable laws.

Buyer may provide

  • Account or identification information
  • Company details where applicable
  • Purchase requirements and specifications
  • Purchase order where applicable
  • Delivery information
  • Import permits or licences where required

Supplier may provide

  • Quotation
  • Proforma invoice
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Product certificates where applicable
  • Certificate of origin where applicable

Trade process documents

  • Inspection or QC report
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Insurance documentation
  • Customs documentation
  • Shipping instructions
  • Other destination-specific documents
Inspection & evidence

Check before the goods leave the origin.

Where available and appropriate, a buyer can request inspection or verification before shipment. VTG's agent network is designed to support physical checks in China and South Korea.

What can be checked

Depending on the assignment: quantity, visible condition, packaging, product appearance and whether the goods generally match agreed specifications.

Evidence

Photographs, inspection notes and other agreed evidence can be attached to the relevant trade record.

Use the evidence properly

Inspection findings should be reviewed against the agreed order specifications. An inspection is evidence, not an absolute guarantee of future performance.

Shipping & customs

The order continues after payment.

Once goods are ready, the trade may move through freight booking, export handling, ports or airports, customs, import clearance and final delivery.

Origin

Goods are prepared, packed, documented and handed into the agreed logistics process.

Movement

Shipment information can connect the commercial order to the physical journey through ports, vessels, airports and routes.

Destination

Customs and other regulatory requirements are handled according to the destination and product, followed by delivery.

Good trade practice

Simple habits that protect a transaction.

Confirm important terms in writing before payment.
Make sure product specifications are precise.
Confirm MOQ, quantity and total value.
Understand shipping responsibilities and delivery terms.
Keep copies of invoices, orders and supporting documents.
Check whether permits or product-specific requirements apply.
Consider samples and inspection for appropriate orders.
Use the correct official channels for sensitive information.
Raise discrepancies promptly through the appropriate VTG process.
Do not assume every trade follows the same document or payment process.
Frequently asked

Quick answers before you trade.

Can I order a small quantity?

Possibly. The supplier and product determine the MOQ. Ask whether samples, trial quantities or smaller batches are available.

Do I need to buy in bulk?

No. VTG can support enquiries for different order sizes, but the supplier's MOQ, pricing and production requirements still apply.

Does every trade require the same documents?

No. Document requirements depend on the product, origin, destination, transport method, transaction and applicable regulations.

Should I request an inspection?

For suitable transactions, especially higher-value or higher-risk orders, a pre-shipment inspection can provide additional evidence before goods move.

Can VTG tell me exactly which documents I need?

VTG can help guide you through the transaction, but final legal, customs and regulatory requirements depend on the relevant authorities, product and destination.

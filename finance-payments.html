VTG connects the commercial, financial, verification and logistics parts of a cross-border transaction. A typical flow can move from trade value and payment terms to financing, payment, verification, shipment, documents and final settlement.

VTG may coordinate information, workflow and participating providers, but it is not automatically the lender, bank, insurer, customs authority or payment processor for every transaction.

Payment arrangements depend on the contract, supplier, product, order value, currency, delivery terms and participating financial provider. A transaction may use a deposit, milestone payment, full payment, documentary payment or another agreed structure.

  • Confirm the payment terms before committing funds.
  • Confirm the amount and currency.
  • Verify the beneficiary and payment instructions.
  • Keep the invoice and payment reference.
  • Confirm when payment is considered received and what happens if it fails or is delayed.

Different payment methods shift control, timing and risk between the parties.

Bank transfer / T/TDirect bank payment. Simple and widely used, but the payer must carefully verify the beneficiary and payment instructions.
Letter of CreditA bank-based documentary mechanism. It can provide structured payment conditions, but requires strict compliance with its terms and documents.
Documentary CollectionBanks handle documents under agreed instructions, but the level of payment protection depends on the collection structure and buyer's obligations.
Escrow-type arrangementsWhere legally and commercially available, a third party may hold funds subject to defined release conditions.
Multi-currency settlementAllows payment and settlement in supported currencies, subject to conversion costs, provider availability and FX exposure.
Digital assetsPotential future or limited options may be considered only where legally permitted and supported by appropriate regulated arrangements.

Trade finance can help eligible businesses fund purchases, production, imports or working capital. VTG may help connect or coordinate a financing workflow, but financing approval belongs to the participating financial institution.

  • Buyer financing
  • Supplier financing
  • Import finance
  • Working-capital finance
  • Letter of Credit financing
  • Documentary finance
  • Structured trade finance
Approval may depend on creditworthiness, transaction quality, compliance checks, collateral or security, repayment capacity and the financial institution's risk policies.

International trade can involve NGN, CNY, KRW, USD and other currencies. The order currency, payment currency and final settlement currency do not always have to be the same, subject to the available arrangement.

  • Exchange-rate movement can change the effective cost of a trade.
  • Conversion fees and spreads can affect the final amount.
  • Timing between quotation, payment and settlement can create FX exposure.
  • A currency mismatch can create additional financial risk.
Normally, the party exposed to the currency movement carries that FX risk unless the contract or financial arrangement allocates it differently.

Good trade infrastructure does not eliminate financial risk. It helps participants identify, manage, document and respond to it.

  • Fraud: fake invoices, impersonation, phishing or fraudulent bank-detail changes.
  • Payment risk: wrong beneficiary, wrong amount, delayed or failed payment.
  • Commercial risk: supplier delay, non-performance or goods not matching agreed terms.
  • Currency risk: exchange-rate movement between agreement and settlement.
  • Regulatory risk: compliance checks, restrictions or payment holds.
  • Counterparty risk: a buyer, supplier or other party fails to perform.

VTG may help coordinate, document, communicate and connect participants, but does not automatically assume the underlying commercial, financial, regulatory, cargo or counterparty risk of every transaction.

Goods remain exposed to physical and transit risks after payment and before final delivery. Depending on the trade, consider marine cargo insurance, air cargo insurance or other appropriate cover.

  • Loss, theft or physical damage
  • Transit incidents
  • General average where applicable
  • Policy limits and exclusions
  • Who arranges insurance
  • When cargo risk transfers under the agreed shipping terms

Insurance should be assessed against the value and nature of the goods, route and contractual responsibilities.

Customs and regulatory problems can create additional cost, delay, penalties or shipment holds.

  • Incorrect tariff classification
  • Incorrect customs valuation
  • Restricted or controlled goods
  • Missing permits or licences
  • Incorrect or incomplete documentation
  • Customs inspection or detention
  • Unexpected duties, taxes or charges
Importers, exporters and their appointed customs/logistics professionals remain responsible for complying with applicable laws and requirements.

Depending on the transaction, users or providers may request information such as:

  • Proforma invoice
  • Commercial invoice
  • Purchase order
  • Buyer and supplier/company information
  • Shipping documents
  • Inspection or quality reports
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Bank or payment information
  • Import/export documentation
  • Financing and supporting financial documents where applicable

Requirements vary by product, country, transaction value, financial provider and applicable regulations.

  • Confirm supplier and buyer identity.
  • Confirm product, quantity and final price.
  • Confirm the payment and settlement currency.
  • Verify the beneficiary independently.
  • Confirm invoices and transaction references.
  • Understand payment and shipping terms.
  • Consider inspection and insurance where appropriate.
  • Keep transaction records and supporting documents.
Important: Never rely solely on an email requesting changed bank-account details. Independently verify any change through an appropriate trusted channel before sending funds.

VTG may review activity that appears inconsistent with normal trading behaviour or presents a serious security, fraud, identity or payment concern.

  • Fraudulent or altered documents
  • Identity misuse or impersonation
  • Payment manipulation
  • Unauthorized transactions
  • Compromised accounts
  • Suspicious beneficiary changes
  • False or misleading information

Users should report suspected fraud promptly and preserve relevant messages, invoices, payment records and other evidence.

Where VTG detects or receives a report of suspected fraud, payment manipulation, identity misuse, unauthorized activity, document irregularities or another serious transaction concern, VTG may temporarily restrict or suspend the relevant account(s) and transaction activity while the matter is investigated.

  • The restriction may remain while VTG and, where necessary, a bank, payment provider, logistics provider, inspection provider or other competent party reviews the matter.
  • VTG may request additional documents, explanations, identity information, transaction records or other evidence.
  • A temporary suspension does not by itself mean that the account holder has committed wrongdoing.
  • Access or transaction activity may be restored when the review is satisfactorily resolved.
  • Where misconduct, fraud, serious rule breaches or unlawful activity are established, VTG may permanently restrict or close the account, cancel or suspend transactions and refer the matter to relevant authorities where appropriate or required.

VTG can provide visibility, verification workflows, documentation support, communication, trade intelligence and connections between participants and relevant service providers.

VTG does not automatically guarantee a supplier's performance, a buyer's payment, financing approval, cargo safety, insurance recovery, customs clearance, exchange rates or the performance of another independent provider.

The exact responsibility of each party depends on the transaction, contracts, applicable law and the participating regulated or service providers.
  • Never rush a payment because of pressure or an unexpected request.
  • Verify beneficiary details before transferring funds.
  • Keep invoices, quotations, contracts and payment evidence.
  • Understand the shipping terms and responsibility for insurance.
  • Consider inspection for appropriate higher-value or higher-risk orders.
  • Understand your FX exposure before agreeing to a currency.
  • Understand financing costs and repayment obligations before accepting finance.
  • Use appropriate regulated banks and payment providers.

Can VTG arrange financing?

VTG may connect or coordinate eligible financing workflows, but approval belongs to the participating financial institution.

Who carries FX risk?

Normally the party exposed to the currency movement, unless the contract or financial arrangement allocates that risk differently.

Is every payment protected?

No payment method removes every risk. The level and type of protection depends on the method, provider, contract and circumstances.

Can my account be suspended?

Yes. VTG may temporarily restrict an account or transaction when there is a serious security, fraud, identity, compliance or transaction concern while an investigation is conducted.

What if I disagree with a transaction decision?

Use the applicable VTG support or dispute process and provide the relevant records and evidence.
